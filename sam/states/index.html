Where your state keeps its entity records

Every state's business-entity search, and what each state calls the good-standing document. Companion to Appendix H of The SAM.gov Registration Handbook. Current as of September 2026; corrections welcome.

Read the address before you type anything. The book's rule is that a government address ends in .gov. State portals are the exception that proves it: most do, but 7 of the 51 below run on .us, .org or .com domains and are still the state. Those rows are marked. Match the address on your screen to the one printed here, from the right-hand end, and enter nothing on a page that doesn't match.

What to look up, once

Appendix H is a worksheet. From your state's page, record: the exact legal name and address as the state holds them (Row 2 of the canonical string); whether the filing shows a separate effective date; the name and cost of the good-standing document and how long banks treat it as current; whether a certified copy of the articles is a separate order; and the annual-report deadline and late fee.
